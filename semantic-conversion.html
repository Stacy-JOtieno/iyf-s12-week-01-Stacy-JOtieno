REASONS FOR THE FREQUENT BUILDING COLLAPSE IN KENYA

Developers frequently cut costs by using counterfeit materials, weak steel reinforcement bars, or incorrect concrete-to-sand ratios. Investigators have noted cases where cheap timber supports were used instead of steel props to hold massive concrete slabs

Adequate foundations can account for a massive portion of construction costs. To save money, many developers bypass soil testing. This is particularly dangerous in Nairobi, where much of the terrain consists of black cotton soil, a clay-rich deposit that shrinks and swells aggressively, causing structural shifts and sudden foundation failure if left untreated

Many developers hire uncertified local artisans (jua kali fundis) rather than registered engineers to save money on wages. Surveys of built-environment professionals show that the use of unqualified individuals for construction (72.3%) and supervision (66.0%) are the leading catalysts for collapse.
